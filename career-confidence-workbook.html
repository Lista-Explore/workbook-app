The Career Confidence Assessment Workbook

Module 3: Build Your Resilience Toolkit

Career confidence isn’t just about feeling good or having a general sense of self-assurance. It’s about believing in your ability to succeed in specific professional situations: speaking up in meetings, making important decisions, adapting to change, or advocating for yourself.

This workbook holds everything you record in the Career Confidence Assessment: your ratings across four professional areas, your confidence profile, your reflections on what sits behind your scores, and your commitment to action. Keep it as a snapshot of where you are today, and come back to it whenever you want to see how far your confidence has grown.

What this assessment measures

This idea comes from Albert Bandura’s Self-Efficacy Theory, which shows that our belief in our capabilities directly influences how we approach challenges, stay motivated, and persist through setbacks. Bandura describes four sources of self-efficacy: mastery experiences, vicarious experiences, social persuasion, and physiological and emotional states.

This assessment does not measure your worth, your intelligence, or your potential. It measures your current beliefs about your own capabilities. Your scores reflect your history, not your ceiling. Come with curiosity, not judgment.

Worth knowing

These scores are a snapshot of your current beliefs—shaped by your experiences so far. They are not fixed, nor are they a measure of your potential. Every area where you score lower is one where your resilience work can deepen your confidence. Every area where you score higher is evidence that it is already working.

Part 3: Guiding Reflection

Use these questions to explore your results more deeply. Keep your scores from Part 2 in front of you as you write.

Name your strongest area. Think about past successes, skills, supportive environments, or mentors that have helped you feel strong in this area. What has made this area easier for you than others? Can you trace it back to a mastery experience: a moment when you succeeded at something that built your belief in your ability?

Name your lowest area. Identify beliefs, fears, past experiences, or specific patterns that might be limiting you, for example: fear of making the wrong choice, discomfort with conflict, or uncertainty during change. Which of Bandura’s four sources is most relevant here? What would it look like to start building that source?

Start small. For example: volunteering to lead a short part of a meeting, making one proactive decision without overthinking it, practicing asking for feedback from someone you trust, or seeking out a mentor who has navigated a similar challenge.

Part 5: Your Commitment to Action

Look back at the suggestions you got from the AI prompt in Part 4. Not every idea will be the right fit right now, and that’s completely fine. The goal isn’t to tackle everything. It’s about picking one or two actions that feel both meaningful and achievable, and committing to trying them.

Confidence grows with practice, just as resilience does. Every action you take creates a new mastery experience, which means every step forward also deepens your confidence for what comes after.
